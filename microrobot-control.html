Project overview

This ongoing research explores three-dimensional motion control of magnetic microrobots for dental applications. The initial work uses a handheld device that enables basic 3D microrobot motion while the device remains stationary. Further work integrates a robotic arm and visual tracking to improve control performance and practical usability.

Initial stage: Handheld device

A 3D printed three coil device was developed to enable three dimensional motion of microrobots. The coils are inclined and arranged at 60° to one another, with each coil independently controlled by a programmable power supply. They generate magnetic forces and torques from three directions separately, enabling the microrobot swarm to perform wheel-like rotational motion.

Current stage: Robotic arm control and visual tracking

The structure of the handheld device limits its performance in practical applications. To address this limitation, a robotic arm is introduced to hold and move the coil device, while AprilTags are used to track the position of the coil holder. Future work will focus on building an integrated system to compare the camera-tracked position of the coil holder with the microrobot position obtained from CBCT
